🌱 First steps

What the robot needs before it can draw anything.

  1. 1Plug inConnect the robot's USB cable to the laptop and switch the robot on.
  2. 2Pick the portOpen ⚙ Settings in the top-left and choose the serial port under Connection. On Windows it looks like COM3; if only one port is listed, that is the robot.
  3. 3Set the speedSettings → Speed. Low (default) suits most powders. Use Aqua Low for very fine powder that runs quickly.
  4. 4Fill the funnelPut the rangoli powder in the funnel and check it flows when the valve opens.
  5. 5Draw somethingPlace a design on the canvas, then press Send to Robot in the bottom strip.

Good to know

Nothing is sent to the robot until you press Send to Robot. Placing and moving designs on the canvas is always safe.

If no port appears, unplug and replug the cable, then re-open Settings — the list is re-read every time it opens.

🖼 Pre-designed gallery

Ready-made rangolis, your saved designs, and your digitized notebook pages, all in one place.

  1. 1Open itClick ✎ Designs on the canvas, then Pre-designed. The gallery also opens on its own when the app starts.
  2. 2Pick a designClick any card. The design lands on the canvas ready to be moved.
  3. 3Place itDrag it where you want it on the mat. The grid behind the canvas is the real drawing area.
  4. 4Save your ownAnything on the canvas can be saved into ★ My Designs with the 💾 Save button, and it comes back here.

Good to know

✕ on a card deletes a saved design. Built-in designs cannot be deleted.

✋ on a notebook page lays down that page's pulli dots instead of its lines — see Pulli Mode.

✨ AI Generated

Describe a rangoli in words and have one drawn for you. This is the one feature that needs internet.

  1. 1Open it✎ Designs → AI Generated.
  2. 2Answer the quizA few quick questions — occasion, mood, how full you want it. Or type your own description instead.
  3. 3Wait for the sketchThe design is generated, then traced into strokes the robot can draw. This takes a few seconds.
  4. 4Keep or redoIf you do not like it, generate another. Nothing is sent to the robot until you press Send.

Good to know

Needs an internet connection and an OpenAI key in openai_key.txt. Everything else in this app works offline.

At a venue with no Wi-Fi, use Import Designs or the Notebook Digitizer instead — both run entirely on the laptop.

📁 Import Designs

Bring in a DXF drawing made elsewhere.

  1. 1Open it✎ Designs → Import Designs.
  2. 2Choose a filePick a .dxf file. Polylines and arcs are read; text and hatching are ignored.
  3. 3Check the previewThe preview shows what will be drawn and how many strokes it is. Parts can be dropped here before anything is placed.
  4. 4ConfirmConfirm Import puts it on the canvas like any other design.

Good to know

Runs entirely on this laptop — no internet, nothing uploaded.

A design far bigger than the mat is scaled to fit the drawable area, so it is never sent off the edge.

🔧 Robot Test

Simple shapes for checking the machine — powder flow, squareness, whether the valve opens cleanly.

  1. 1Pick a shape⚙ Settings → Robot Test, then choose Line, Square, Circle, Flower and so on.
  2. 2Click the canvasClick where the shape should go. A Line needs two clicks: start point, then end point.
  3. 3Send itPress Send to Robot. Watch the first few centimetres — that is where a flow problem shows.
  4. 4Back to SelectSet Robot Test back to Select when you are done, or every canvas click keeps adding shapes.

Good to know

A square that comes out as a parallelogram usually means a loose belt, not a software problem.

Use Line first when trying a new powder: it is the quickest way to see whether the flow is even.

🎓 Learn Mode

The robot and the learner take turns: it draws a part, you copy it by hand with the powder bottle.

  1. 1Start itFeatures → Learn Mode → Start.
  2. 2Choose a designPick a built-in design or a page from a digitized notebook.
  3. 3Watch the robotThe robot draws its share of the parts. How much it draws depends on the level — about 70% at Level 1, none at all at Level 5.
  4. 4Take your turnCopy the next part by hand with the powder bottle, then press the button to say you are done.
  5. 5Photograph itAt the end, photograph the finished rangoli with the USB camera. It is scored, and the level moves up as the learner improves.

Good to know

The level is remembered between sessions in learner_profile.json — nobody restarts at the beginning every day.

Stars, the mascot and confetti are always on: the screens are built for a child, but nothing about the machine changes.

No camera? Continue → skips the photo. The lesson still counts.

✋ Pulli Mode

The robot lays down only the dot grid. Every line is drawn by hand — the traditional way to learn a kolam.

  1. 1Get a dot grid✋ on any notebook page in the gallery, or reach Level 5 in Learn Mode.
  2. 2Let the robot dotThe robot lays the pulli and stops. Those are the grandmother's own dots, off her own page — not dots invented by the app.
  3. 3Draw the linesJoin the dots by hand with the powder bottle. The robot draws nothing else.
  4. 4Photograph itPhotograph the result to have it scored, the same as any other lesson.

Good to know

Only pages that had their dots detected when they were digitized offer ✋.

This is the mode to hand an elder: the dots are a scaffold, the kolam is still hers.

📖 Notebook Digitizer

Photograph the pages of a hand-written kolam puthagam and turn each page into strokes the robot can draw.

  1. 1Open itFeatures → Kolam Notebook. The notebooks you have already done are listed first.
  2. 2Open or importOpen reads a notebook you already digitized. Import pages adds more pages to it; ＋ Import a new notebook starts a new one.
  3. 3Photograph a pageHold the page flat under the camera, or upload a photo taken on a phone. Good even light matters more than a good camera.
  4. 4Check the tracingThe dots and lines found on the page are shown for review. Anything picked up in error can be removed before saving.
  5. 5Save and continueSave the page and go straight on to the next one — a whole book goes in one sitting.

Good to know

Entirely offline: OpenCV on this laptop reads the page. Her book never leaves the room.

Pages are numbered automatically and carry on from where the last sitting stopped.

Saved pages show up in the gallery, in Learn Mode, and in Kolam of the Day.

🚨 If something is wrong

The short list of things that actually go wrong, and what to do about each.

  1. 1No serial port listedReplug the USB cable and re-open Settings. The port list is re-read each time.
  2. 2Robot does not moveCheck it is powered on, then check the port is the right one. </> Debug Log in the banner shows what was sent and what came back.
  3. 3Lines are faint or brokenPowder problem, not software: check the funnel is not bridged and try a slower speed.
  4. 4Camera not found🔍 Scan for USB cameras again. A camera unplugged since last time is forgotten automatically.
  5. 5AI will not generateThat is the only online feature. With no internet, use Import Designs or the Notebook Digitizer.

Good to know

⬛ </> Debug Log in the banner is the first place to look for anything machine-related.

Nothing is ever sent to the robot without pressing Send to Robot.
